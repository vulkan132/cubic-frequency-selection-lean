## External-source check and precise scope

The source was checked directly: [Green--Tao, The quantitative behaviour
of polynomial orbits on nilmanifolds](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0709.3562),
Definitions 2.3, 2.4 and A.7, Propositions A.9 and A.10. Reduced rational
height is max of the absolute numerator and positive denominator.
The weak-basis conditions combine bounded rational Lie coefficients with
both logarithmic lattice inclusions under one bound. An adapted Malcev
basis additionally requires appropriate rational filtration subgroups;
the subgroup version also requires matching the subgroup and its lattice.
None of those external construction propositions is assumed or proved in
the new modules.

## Actual original weak-basis data

OriginalWeakBasisBound records native exponential subgroup uniqueness,
actual Mathlib Lie-bracket coefficients of the original tangent basis,
their reduced rational heights, and both inclusions for the entire
original lattice with 0 < den <= Q and 2 < Q. The exponential coordinates
are identified by the native subgroup property established at F61.
The record does not disguise a coordinate algebra as the actual Lie algebra.

For a fixed rational coefficient tensor C and lattice denominator den,
originalWeakBasisHeight is the explicit finite maximum of 3, den and all
reduced heights of C. The generic constructor supplies every record field
from actual native data. Its monotonicity theorem allows extra fixed
structural costs to be included in the same Q.

original_full_bounded_weak_basis constructs this record from the original
rational joint law, strict triangular law, full original integer-grid
lattice and rational original observation basis. The same selected full
integer/real basis supports the actual global exponential/logarithm,
their smoothness, the literal rational arrays S and T, and logCoord(1)=0.
The common Q also bounds the degrees and every coefficient height in
both arrays. Coefficients outside their supports are included.

The quantifier order is fixed original presentation, then the constructed
bases, maps and Q, then all real inputs, all original lattice elements
and all integer vectors. No bound is imposed on the original real
observation coefficients. There is no claim that Q depends only on
dimension or degree across arbitrary rational presentations.

## Finite rational families and actual linear kernels

An explicit reduced numerator/denominator grid proves that rationals of
bounded height form a finite set. Finite coordinate arrays inherit that
property. Consequently, a fixed transformation on bounded-height rational
arrays has a uniform output-height bound, obtained from that finite
input family. This is not a polynomial-in-height estimate and does not
supply a height bound for inputs generated by a general descent.

rationalBasisFunctional applies a rational covector to the actual
coordinates in a fixed original real basis. Evaluation on each basis
vector recovers its original coefficient. A nonzero covector gives a
proper linear kernel; bounded-height covectors give finitely many such
actual kernels. This is a statement about submodules of the original
space. It does not by itself identify a Lie ideal, an integrated subgroup,
its original lattice intersection, or a new rational presentation.
